# FoodWise AI research and implementation review

Reviewed 9 October 2026. This is a targeted engineering review, not an exhaustive systematic review of every publication or repository. Searches covered canteen demand, attendance, stochastic preparation, censored sales, plate-waste interventions, campus feedback, prediction uncertainty and ingredient stock management. Primary publications and source repositories were preferred. Access depth is explicit below: an abstract is not treated as a full-paper review. Original pack specifications and data remain the source of truth.

## Decisions implemented

| Need | Research input | Implemented adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Balance leftover and service costs | Newsvendor literature and censored-demand study | Backend empirical loss minimization; manager penalty assumptions, capacity and historical shortage-frequency constraints; no recommendation when infeasible; explicit manager adoption and immutable approval provenance. |
| Detect when served food hides demand | Censored newsvendor study and reproduction code | Optional staff shortage flag in actuals/CSV; unknown stays unknown; optimization blocked if selected history contains confirmed shortages. No fabricated lost-demand estimate. |
| Establish whether complexity helps | Canteen GAM paper, forecasting text, StatsForecast | Five models on identical rolling observations; MAE, RMSE, signed bias and WAPE; fixed NumPy calendar ridge challenger; training IDs; P0 default retained. |
| Explain uncertainty honestly | Canteen uncertainty discussion, MAPIE tutorial | Actual rolling coverage and width of existing descriptive ranges. No conformal guarantee or new confidence-interval claim. |
| Turn portion advice into measurement | Portion-intervention studies and campus FoodWise paper | Manager-approved prospective protocols and clearly labeled retrospective comparisons; actual/history scopes separated; source filtering; attendance-weighted plate/consumed/served/untouched grams per diner; distinct services, duplicate exclusion, evidence and event/shortage context. |

All feature calculations are server-owned. SQLite stores optimization decisions and measurement protocols. Existing safety review, permanent plate-waste redistribution block, atomic imports and offline explanations continue to work. No new runtime dependency or provider connection was added.

## Scholarly sources and what was read

1. **Posch, Truden, Hungerländer & Pilz — A Bayesian Approach for Predicting Food and Beverage Sales in Staff Canteens and Restaurants (2021 revision).** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.12647). Read problem assumptions, trend/seasonality regularization, model and interval construction, temporal evaluation design and conclusion. The study uses POS item counts across two roughly twenty-month datasets; our response is continuous cooked served kg per diner. Calendar effects and regularization motivate an experimental ridge comparator, not a replication of its Bayesian GAM. Monthly/yearly seasonality, negative-binomial count likelihood and published accuracy are not transferred. Its discussion of approximate interval coverage reinforces our descriptive labeling.

2. **Hssaine & Sinclair — The Data-Driven Censored Newsvendor Problem (2026 v3 of 2024 preprint).** [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01763v3). Read introduction, formulation and critical quantile, censoring/observable boundary discussion, computational experiment sections and the companion algorithm source. This motivates asymmetric overage/underage decisions and the shortage guard. We implement uncensored empirical sample loss, not their robust censored algorithm or theorem. Confirmed stockouts cannot reveal the unobserved demand tail. Unknown flags remain a limitation. Our discrete inverse empirical CDF and explicit loss frontier avoid assuming that interpolating a sample quantile exactly minimizes the piecewise-linear empirical objective.

3. **Berkowitz et al. — Reduced-portion entrées in a worksite and restaurant setting: impact on food consumption and waste (2016).** [Publisher full PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E3AA309286FF382A7A46323775331098/S1368980016001348a.pdf/reducedportion_entrees_in_a_worksite_and_restaurant_setting_impact_on_food_consumption_and_waste.pdf). Read design, weighing procedures, results, discussion and limitations. Reduced options were added alongside full portions in two establishments. Differences in consumption and waste motivate measuring both rather than assuming that less waste means adequate intake. Our kitchen-level per-diner comparison is an adaptation, not the paper's individual-plate experiment. Optional seconds remain available; no nutrient or causal claim is computed.

4. **Yu et al. — FoodWise: Food Waste Reduction and Behavior Change on Campus with Data Visualization and Gamification (2023).** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.12882). Read dashboard architecture, campaign evaluation and discussion. It combines visual feedback with a campus action campaign; its survey/action outcomes do not establish our measured mass savings. We adopt the evidence-to-action measurement loop. Cameras, rewards, accounts and action-to-kg conversion are not adopted. **This is prior work with the same FoodWise name. Our independently built hackathon prototype is not affiliated with that system; the name and general idea should not be presented as novel.**

5. **Visschers, Gundlach & Beretta — Smaller servings vs. information provision: Results of two interventions to reduce plate waste in two university canteens (2020).** [Publisher abstract/preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0956053X19308013). Abstract and available preview inspected; full article not obtained. Short-duration, two-canteen evidence motivates distinct information-only and portion protocol options. No published reduction percentage is used as a FoodWise forecast or savings multiplier.

6. **Richardson, Prescott & Ellison — Impact of plate shape and size on individual food waste in a university dining hall (2021).** [Authors' institutional abstract](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/impact-of-plate-shape-and-size-on-individual-food-waste-in-a-univ/). Institutional abstract inspected; full paper not reviewed. It provides another serving-environment intervention candidate. Plate purchasing/shape prescriptions are deferred: this prototype has no tray-level measurements or matched experimental design.

7. **A Conformal Approach to Feature-based Newsvendor under Model Misspecification (arXiv:2412.13159).** [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13159). Abstract inspected, not a full derivation review. Candidate for future calibrated decision research. No conformal algorithm is implemented or claimed; ninety synthetic days and sparse weekday/event samples do not justify a distribution-free guarantee.

8. **Hyndman & Athanasopoulos — Forecasting: Principles and Practice, third edition.** Read [time-series cross-validation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html) and [simple forecasting methods](https://otexts.com/fpp3/simple-methods.html). Direct basis for earlier-only rolling evaluation and seasonal naive benchmark. Our weekday baseline uses the latest matching calendar weekday, with latest-observation fallback if absent, and scales served/diner by known evaluation attendance. Closed days are not inserted as zero demand. Evaluation is conditional on known attendance, not a test of attendance prediction.

9. **Rossi — Inventory Analytics (2021), Stochastic Inventory Control.** [Publisher chapter record](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0252/chapters/10.11647/obp.0252.04). Chapter description inspected; attempted full university PDF retrieval failed. It is a further reading pointer for service constraints and inventory policies, not a full-chapter review or source for copied code. The implemented newsvendor formula is supported directly by source 2.

Result: extra complexity does not consistently improve this synthetic sample. Updated belief: attendance normalization is useful here; automatic model promotion would be premature. P0 remains the operational default. Historical 10–90 percentile ranges do not reliably attain 80% rolling coverage, and remain labeled descriptive. Actual attendance is known for all evaluations.

With explicit illustrative shortage penalty ₹180/kg, surplus penalty equal to historical item cost, a 20% historical shortage-frequency cap and the provided simulation capacity, empirical choices were Rice **40.4274 kg**, Dal **20.3726 kg**, curry **25.5416 kg**. Each has 12.5% historical shortage frequency, reflecting eight samples. These are projected manager scenarios, not field-validated recommendations or measured savings. All eight selected observations lack shortage flags.

The synthetic retrospective Rice comparison displayed 23.80 versus 24.62 g plate waste per diner. It shows an increase rather than manufacturing a reduction. Different event counts and synthetic source are visible. No intervention was performed and no causal attribution is made.

Verification: original P0 plus new mathematical, constraint, leakage, provenance, trial and CSV compatibility tests; frontend type/build; live browser adoption/approval/report, capacity failure, prospective empty trial and synthetic retrospective comparison. Exact final results are in [RESEARCH_VALIDATION.md](RESEARCH_VALIDATION.md).

## Next evidence that could change these decisions

Collect weighed, item-level kitchen records with shortage flags and an explicit meal-service identifier. Pre-register a prospective holdout and manager costs; compare point forecasts and preparation outcomes before promoting a challenger. For portions, collect equivalent service windows with consistent menus, weighing and optional seconds, plus an appropriate comparison design; observe consumed food and diner feedback. Real improvements on a separate future evaluation would justify changing the model. Robust coverage on representative data and checked assumptions could justify calibrated uncertainty. Verified recipe yields and storage/allergen procedures could justify stock-aware recipe planning. No paper's published effect size is assumed to transfer automatically.
